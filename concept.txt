# PROJECT: Multiplayer Mobile Party Game — Confidence Trivia

## 1. ROLE

You are an expert full-stack mobile game developer, multiplayer/backend engineer, UI/UX designer, and product architect.

Your job is to help me build a **real, playable mobile multiplayer game** based on the specification below.

Do not redesign the core game concept unless I explicitly ask you to.

You may make sensible technical and UX decisions where the specification leaves room for them, but preserve the gameplay rules, scoring philosophy, and multiplayer flow described here.

The goal is to build a polished **mobile-first multiplayer game for friend groups**, initially targeting approximately 4–12+ simultaneous players.

The game should eventually be capable of expanding to PC/web, but **do not build the PC/web client yet**. However, choose a backend/game-state architecture that will make adding a web client later straightforward.

---

# 2. GAME CONCEPT

The core idea is:

> **Knowing the answer is only half the game. Knowing how confident you should be is the other half.**

Players answer questions simultaneously.

After answering, they can optionally commit a confidence level.

Confidence increases the reward for being correct, but also increases the penalty for being wrong.

Players then briefly see **everyone else's confidence levels**, without seeing their answers.

Each player can make **one optional side bet** on another player's answer.

The correct answer is revealed.

Scores, confidence results, side bets, and streaks are processed.

Then the next round begins.

The game should feel:

* Fast
* Competitive
* Social
* Easy to understand
* Slightly chaotic
* Strategic without being complicated
* Fun with both knowledgeable and average players
* Particularly fun with a recurring friend group

The game should NOT feel like a slow traditional board game where players take individual turns.

Almost all actions should happen simultaneously.

# 7. CORE ROUND LOOP

Every normal round follows this structure:

### STEP 1 — QUESTION

All players receive the same question.

Example:

> Which planet is closest to the Sun?

Players answer privately.

Depending on the question type, the answer may be:

* Multiple choice
* Text input
* Number
* Ordering
* True/false
* Word/letter input
* Image-based selection
* Other supported question formats

---

### STEP 2 — OPTIONAL CONFIDENCE

After answering, players can optionally choose a confidence level.

Available confidence values:

> **1 / 3 / 5 / 6**

There is also:

> **NO CONFIDENCE**

Confidence is optional.

A player who does not want to risk points can skip confidence entirely.

IMPORTANT:

Do not automatically assign confidence 1.

"No confidence" and "confidence 1" are separate choices.

The UI should make this distinction very clear.

---

# 8. CONFIDENCE SCORING

Use the following initial scoring system:

| Confidence    | Correct | Wrong |
| ------------- | ------: | ----: |
| No confidence |      +0 |    +0 |
| 1             |      +1 |    -1 |
| 3             |      +2 |    -2 |
| 5             |      +3 |    -4 |
| 6             |      +4 |    -5 |

This is the initial balancing configuration.

Do not arbitrarily change these values.

However, structure the code so these values are configurable later for balancing and playtesting.

For example, they should come from a central configuration rather than being scattered throughout the codebase.

---

# 9. INTERPRETATION OF CONFIDENCE

Confidence represents:

> "How strongly am I willing to stand behind my answer?"

It is not merely a bonus multiplier.

Players should feel that choosing 6 is a genuine gamble.

Examples:

### No confidence

Correct:

> +0

Wrong:

> +0

Meaning:

> "I don't want to risk anything."

### Confidence 1

Correct:

> +1

Wrong:

> -1

Small risk.

### Confidence 3

Correct:

> +2

Wrong:

> -2

Moderate commitment.

### Confidence 5

Correct:

> +3

Wrong:

> -4

Serious commitment.

### Confidence 6

Correct:

> +4

Wrong:

> -5

Maximum confidence / maximum gamble.

---

# 10. BASE SCORING

For the initial game:

A correct answer with no confidence:

> +0

A wrong answer with no confidence:

> +0

Confidence modifies this according to the table above.

Some special question types may have a different base value, such as +2 for particularly difficult or special questions.

Do not introduce complicated scoring multipliers unless explicitly requested.

The scoring system should remain easy for players to understand.

# 11. CONFIDENCE BOARD

# 12. SIDE BETTING

Each player may make:

## ONE optional side bet per round.

The player can select another player from the confidence board.

They then choose:

> **CORRECT**

or

> **WRONG**

The side bet always has a value of:

> **+1 / -1**

Example:

Player selects:

> John — Confidence 5

Then chooses:

> WRONG

If John is wrong:

> Side bettor gets +1

If John is correct:

> Side bettor gets -1

If the player predicts CORRECT:

John correct:

> +1

John wrong:

> -1

---

# 13. SIDE BET RULES

Important:

* Side betting is optional.
* Maximum one side bet per player per round.
* A player cannot side bet themselves.
* A player may skip the side bet.
* Side bets should be extremely fast.
* Do NOT create a separate "challenge phase".
* Do NOT make players wait through sequential turns.
* Do NOT require confirmation screens unless necessary to prevent accidental taps.

The side bet should feel like:

> "I think you're bluffing."

rather than a complicated secondary game.

---

# 14. WHY SIDE BETTING EXISTS

Side betting serves two purposes:

### Strategic purpose

It provides a small additional way to gain or lose points.

### Social purpose

It forces players to pay attention to the other players.

The game should encourage players to develop opinions such as:

> "John always overestimates his confidence."

> "Sarah only chooses 6 when she actually knows."

> "Mike is bluffing again."

This should emerge naturally from repeated games.

# 15. CONFIDENCE STREAKS

Add a lightweight streak system.

A confidence streak is created when a player:

> Chooses confidence 5 or 6 AND answers correctly.

Examples:

5 → Correct:

> 🔥 Streak 1

6 → Correct:

> 🔥 Streak 2

3 → Correct:

> Streak remains 2

6 → Wrong:

> Streak resets

No-confidence answers do not increase the streak.

Confidence 1 does not increase the streak.

Only successful high-confidence answers count.

---

# 16. STREAK DESIGN

The streak system should NOT become a huge secondary scoring system.

Its purpose is:

* Excitement
* Risk/reward
* Recognition
* Small bonuses
* Encouraging confident play

Initial concept:

### Streak 2

Next successful high-confidence answer can receive:

> +1 streak bonus

### Streak 3

Potentially unlock a small defensive benefit such as:

> Reduce the next confidence penalty by 1.

Higher streaks can primarily be cosmetic/status-based initially.

IMPORTANT:

Keep the streak implementation configurable.

We should be able to disable/change streak rewards during playtesting without rewriting the game.

If playtesting shows the streak rewards make the game too snowbally, reduce or remove the mechanical rewards while keeping the visual streak.

---

# 17. NO TARGET SCORE

There is NO target score.

Do not implement:

> First player to 50 wins.

Instead:

The game ends after the predetermined number of rounds.

---

# 18. COMEBACK PHILOSOPHY

Do not artificially rubber-band the game.

If someone is bad:

> They can lose.

If someone is extremely good:

> They can perform well.

However, because confidence is optional and high-confidence plays can produce large swings, a player who is behind can still recover.

For example:

A player who successfully gets multiple confidence-5/6 answers can rapidly change the leaderboard.

This is intentional.

Do not introduce:

* Automatic catch-up bonuses
* Losing-player multipliers
* Forced handicaps
* Artificial score equalization

unless later playtesting demonstrates a serious balance problem.

---

# 19. KNOWLEDGE VS CONFIDENCE

The game should reward two different skills:

### Knowledge

Knowing the correct answer.

### Risk assessment

Knowing when to trust yourself.

A player who chooses no confidence opts out of both the reward and the penalty for that round.

A less knowledgeable player who correctly recognizes when they know an answer can compete.

This is intentional.

The game is not simply:

> "Who knows the most trivia?"

It is:

> "Who plays the best?"

### Damage star wagers

* The host selects a per-player wager of 5, 15, 40, 60, 75, 100, 150, 300, or 500 stars when creating the room.
* Both players must have enough stars when the match starts.
* Both stakes are deducted atomically into server-authoritative escrow; the match does not start if either payment fails.
* The winner receives the combined two-player pot.
* An exact draw refunds each player's original stake.
* Leaving or failing to reconnect after the grace period counts as a defeat and awards the pot to the remaining player.
* Every stake, payout, and refund is recorded in the immutable star transaction ledger and settled idempotently.

## PHASE 9 — Ranked Mode

Add competitive matchmaking and a persistent rating such as LP only after the core game is stable and player identity is secured.

Account implementation is in progress. Fresh installations receive one persisted `Guest 100–999` identity. Guests can play unranked games but cannot access Shop, Inventory, Ranked, or future friends features. Google and Apple accounts upgrade the existing guest player row so its progression remains intact. Dedicated matchmaking comes after account identity; the current ranked-room prototype requires exactly four players in the meantime.

Required foundations:

* Persistent authenticated accounts rather than device ID alone
* Ranked queue and matchmaking rules
* Placement matches and provisional rating
* Server-authoritative LP gains and losses
* Rank tiers, seasons, and leaderboard
* Disconnect, abandonment, boosting, and duplicate-account protections
* Match history and rating audit records

The initial LP formula below is canonical for the prototype and should be validated with telemetry from stable ranked games before public competitive release.

### Ranked format

* Ranked games use the Classic question and confidence loop.
* Public ranked play is designed for exactly four-player games.
* In the current prototype, Ranked is selected from Create Game and uses the normal room lobby/code flow.
* Ranked rooms require exactly four players to start and are capped at four players.
* Dedicated ranked matchmaking is future work and is not exposed from the leaderboard screen.
* Ranked games use a fixed round count rather than a host-selected length.
* LP is determined only by final placement. Raw in-game points decide placement but never convert directly into LP.
* Before public ranked release, code-based ranked rooms may be replaced by a dedicated four-player queue after account identity is secured.
* Leaving or abandoning a ranked game counts as fourth place.
* LP cannot fall below zero.
* Tied players receive the LP result for their shared placement.

### Ranked LP changes

| Placement | LP change |
| --------- | --------: |
| 1st       |       +30 |
| 2nd       |       +15 |
| 3rd       |         0 |
| 4th       |       -15 |

### Placement matches and Novice status

* New ranked players begin as **Novice**, which means unranked rather than a permanent competitive division.
* Novice players complete three placement matches.
* Placement progress is displayed as 0/3, 1/3, 2/3, then the earned rank after match three.
* Each placement awards internal placement points: 3 for first, 2 for second, 1 for third, and 0 for fourth.
* Placement LP is not shown and normal LP gains/losses do not apply during these three matches.
* Initial rank is based on the sum of the three placement results:

| Placement points | Initial rank |
| ---------------: | ------------ |
| 0-2              | Bronze III   |
| 3                | Bronze II    |
| 4                | Bronze I     |
| 5                | Silver III   |
| 6                | Silver II    |
| 7                | Silver I     |
| 8                | Gold III     |
| 9                | Gold II      |

Gold II is the highest possible initial placement. Higher ranks must be earned through continued ranked play.

### Rank ladder

Every normal division spans 100 LP. Division order runs from III to I:

1. Bronze III, II, I
2. Silver III, II, I
3. Gold III, II, I
4. Platinum III, II, I
5. Diamond III, II, I
6. Master
7. Omniscient

Master begins at 1500 LP. Omniscient begins at 2000 LP and is the highest rank. Master and Omniscient do not have subdivisions.

### Global ranked leaderboard

The ranked leaderboard is a dedicated screen ordered from highest to lowest LP. Each row displays:

* Global leaderboard position
* Player display name
* LP
* Rank/division
* Win count already tracked on the persistent player profile

The client renders the global top 10. If the current player is outside the top 10, their own row is displayed separately below it with their real global position, for example #120. If they are already in the top 10, no duplicate personal row is shown.

### Ranked integrity and future seasons

* Ranked results, placement progress, LP, and leaderboard ordering are server-authoritative.
* Every LP change must have a match-linked audit record so duplicate processing cannot award LP twice.
* Authenticated accounts, seasonal resets, boosting protection, and stronger abandonment enforcement remain required before a public competitive release. Device profiles may be used during development only.

# 46. IMPORTANT GAMEPLAY PRINCIPLES

These are non-negotiable unless I explicitly change them:

### Principle 1

**Simultaneous gameplay.**

Avoid sequential turns.

### Principle 2

**Confidence is optional.**

Players can always choose no confidence.

### Principle 3

**Confidence is a risk/reward mechanic.**

Higher confidence means greater potential reward and greater potential loss.

### Principle 4

**Answers remain hidden until reveal.**

Other players only see confidence before the reveal.

### Principle 5

**Side bets are fast and optional.**

One per player per round.

### Principle 6

**No target score.**

Games end after the set number of rounds.

### Principle 7

**No artificial rubber-banding.**

Good players can win. Bad players can lose.

### Principle 8

**Comebacks are created through risk.**

A player behind can recover through successful high-confidence plays.

### Principle 9

**The game should be fast.**

Target approximately one minute per round.

### Principle 10

**The game should be easy to understand.**

Players should understand the core game after one round.
